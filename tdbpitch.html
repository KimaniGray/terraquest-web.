1. STRUCTURAL CLARIFICATION

De-Risked Sovereign Deployment vs. CIS Mechanics

To align seamlessly with the institutional mandates of the Eastern and Southern African Trade Fund (ESATF) and the broader TDB Group, this addendum clarifies the legal and operational structure of the TRFF-1 facility. It demonstrates exactly how TDB's capital flows directly into an asset-backed sovereign trade facility, completely bypassing any Collective Investment Scheme (CIS) or pooled fund frameworks.

1.1 Asset-Backed Trade Finance

Despite the branding nomenclature "Tsavo Resilience Food Fund", the issuing entity (Tsavo SEZ SPV Limited) is not structured, registered, or operated as a Collective Investment Scheme (CIS), Mutual Fund, or Alternative Investment Fund.

It is a bankruptcy-remote Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) established solely to execute a standard commercial True Sale Master Repurchase Agreement. Capital deployed by TDB is not pooled into variable-yield equity investments. Instead, TDB participates directly as a pari passu Senior Secured Co-Lender. You hold registered debt directly backed by physical commodities originating from a sovereign entity.

1.2 The Role of SIB

Standard Investment Bank (SIB) is one of Kenya's leading Investment Banks. They possess the balance sheet capacity to underwrite up to USD 100 Million and currently manage over Ksh 180 Billion (~USD 1.38 Billion) in assets.

We integrated SIB into this transaction specifically to leverage their scale and CMA license to unlock domestic Kenyan pension funds. SIB utilizes its regulatory framework to aggregate local pension liquidity, which they then deploy to purchase a specific block of the SPV’s Notes. Crucially, TDB’s institutional capital does not flow through SIB, nor is it co-mingled in their funds.

As illustrated below, TDB enters via the "Direct Investment Route," maintaining an independent, senior-secured lending relationship with the SPV and the Corporate Trustee.

2. MANAGING TDB COVENANTS

We recognize TDB’s Credit Risk Committee adheres to standard Loan Market Association (LMA) guidelines. Since TRFF-1 operates as a ring-fenced, asset-backed trade structure rather than a traditional corporate loan, we have engineered the Trust Deed to proactively address TDB’s mandatory covenants without breaking the isolation of the deal. Here is how we map and mitigate standard TDB covenant requirements:

1. Cross-Default
  • DFI Concern: Standard covenants trigger a default if the sovereign defaults on any external debt.
  • TRFF-1 Solution: The Trust Deed includes a strict Non-Recourse Carve-Out. Because this is a ring-fenced trade, default is limited exclusively to the SPV's assets and the Food Reserve Agency's (FRA) failure to repurchase this specific grain. A broader sovereign default (such as on a Eurobond) does not cross-default this facility.
2. Negative Pledge
  • DFI Concern: Prohibiting the Borrower from pledging assets to third parties.
  • TRFF-1 Solution: The SPV honors a strict negative pledge over the collateralized grain and the transaction accounts. However, we embed a carve-out allowing the FRA to continue its normal national operations and raise independent debt for other sovereign programs.
3. Material Adverse Effect (MAE)
  • DFI Concern: Broad triggers allowing lenders to call debt based on macroeconomic shifts.
  • TRFF-1 Solution: To prevent unfair acceleration, MAE definitions are narrowly tied exclusively to either the physical safety of the maize or the legal enforceability of the English Law security documents.
4. Currency Convertibility & Capital Controls
  • DFI Concern: Sovereign capital controls preventing USD from exiting the host country.
  • TRFF-1 Solution: Handled natively by the Offshore Cash Capture mechanism. The deal requires a 60% regional export allocation. Secondary buyers deposit cleared USD directly into the offshore Trustee Collection Account, completely bypassing the Zambian banking system and any sovereign capital controls.
5. Sovereign Interference
  • DFI Concern: The government arbitrarily revokes export permits or seizes assets.
  • TRFF-1 Solution: The transaction requires upfront Sovereign Immunity Waivers and Master Export Permits. If the government revokes these, it is contractually classified as an "Expropriation of Rights." This acts as the immediate, hard trigger for the Political Risk Insurance (PRI) policy to activate and make TDB whole.

3. LENDER PROTECTIONS

By participating via the Direct Investment Route, TDB's capital deployment is completely divorced from any CIS structures and is fortified by the following direct institutional protections:

  • Direct Collateral Tie-In: Every dollar TDB lends is mapped on a strict 1:1 basis to specific, individually serialized Negotiable Warehouse Receipts (WHRs).
  • Offshore Firewall: The True Sale structure is domiciled offshore in Kenya, completely isolating TDB's capital from the sovereign balance sheet and any Zambian national debt restructuring proceedings.
  • First-Loss Buffer: A pre-funded 10% Cash Escrow is deducted upfront and held under the exclusive, non-discretionary control of the Security Trustee to absorb any localized settlement latency.
