Executive Summary

Project Ndegwa translates the USD 63.6M financing request of PineCrest Holdings (the Investee) into an investable, Shariah-compliant, asset-backed deployment model for Golden Nile Trading LLC (the Investor). Capitalizing on zero-tariff market access established under the Kenya-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), the venture targets the 400,000 MT annual GCC beef import market.

The Investee originally proposed a USD 63.6M capital rollout structured across three consecutive stages: Phase 1 (~USD 10.0M across Months 1–24, comprising the Investee's ~USD 7.2M supply-engine request plus an additive USD 2.8M dedicated export processing facility carve-out ratified by ExCo post-setup); Phase 2 (USD 6.0M across Months 25–36) dedicated to wholesale packaging expansion and secondary regional feedlots; and Phase 3 (USD 50.0M) designated for regional aggregation hubs, chartered livestock vessels, and port cold storage.

Project Ndegwa translates the Investee request into a Musharaka partnership, where the parties pool capital to operate the business and share both profits and losses. The investment is executed by setting up enterprise governance and 50/50 equity co-ownership in UAE InvestCo (ADGM or DIFC), capitalized with a nominal USD 50,000 share capital. Corporate authority sits with a 5-member board comprising two Investee appointees, two Investor appointees, and one mutually agreed independent director.

Operational execution is divided between Kenya OpCo and UAE OpCo:

  • Kenya OpCo holds all assets, operational leases, costs, and expenses related to the upstream investment, including 25-year commercial leases over feedlot yards and fodder land, direct ownership of the USD 2.8M abattoir cold chain and processing machinery, finishing cattle batches acquired at the feedlot gate, feed inventory, veterinary clearance, and local operating costs.
  • UAE OpCo houses all downstream investments, costs, and expenses related to executing GCC commercial contracts, including port clearance, cold warehousing, logistics, regional marketing, and local-currency sales distribution.

The two operating companies transact under an established Transfer Pricing Agreement and arm's-length Master Supply & Off-Take Agreement utilizing a cost-plus formula structured to comply with Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) rules and UAE Corporate Tax transfer pricing regulations.

UAE InvestCo

Corporate Domicile

UAE InvestCo is established in Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) with Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) retained as an alternative option as the central holding company. ADGM provides direct application of English Common Law, standard Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) ringfencing, and strategic alignment with UAE national food security initiatives and maritime logistics hubs.

Capital Structure

The capital structure strictly separates enterprise participation and governance from downstream commercial asset deployment and capital payback. There is no conveyance or transfer of existing family land, primary titles, or legacy assets into the corporate platform.

  • Nominal Capital: USD 50,000 divided into 50,000 Ordinary Shares at USD 1.00 par value. Issued as a single class of ordinary shares establishing exact 50/50 parity.
  • Capital Allocation: Deployed project capital operates outside nominal equity and receives priority recovery (Capital + Agreed Return) before general profit distributions.

Board Governance

Five directors: two appointed by the Investee, two appointed by the Investor, and one independent agribusiness specialist mutually agreed by both parties. The mandate establishes enterprise strategy, approves the Annual Operating Plan, ratifies executive appointments, and authorizes major capital expenditures.

Operating Divisions

Execution across UAE InvestCo, Kenya OpCo, and UAE OpCo is organized into three lean core divisions:

  • Commercial & Sales: Seated directly under the Group CEO. Drives GCC commercial off-take contracts, institutional distributor and supermarket accounts, HORECA supply programs, export pricing matrices, volume delivery schedules, customer credit control, and regional marketing.
  • Operations: Directed by the Group COO. End-to-end management of physical and field activities across three asset models:
    • Owned Assets: Direct ownership of processing hardware (modular Halal slaughter floor, carcass hanging chillers, blast freezers, deboning tables, vacuum packaging lines), refrigerated distribution reefers, Pre-Export Isolation (PEI) diagnostic holding pens, and rotating finishing cattle batches acquired at the feedlot gate.
    • Leased Assets: Operational control under 25-year registered commercial land leases covering the Kitengela and Narok feedlot yards and the 500-acre Narok fodder farm, alongside leased temperature-controlled warehousing in KEZAD/JAFZA.
    • Contracted Assets: Toll-processing backup agreements, exclusive Facilities Use Agreement, scheduled air cargo allocations (JKIA), ocean carrier reefer containers (Mombasa), pastoral sourcing networks, and veterinary extension protocols.
  • Finance & Administration: Directed by the Group CFO. Financial controls, livestock batch cost accounting, transfer pricing compliance (KRA and UAE Corporate Tax), statutory audits, corporate secretarial management, entity licensing maintenance, and revenue waterfall sweeps.

Committee Structure

The Board sets up two operational committees with fixed chairs agreed by both parties. Both the Investor and the Investee have board members on each committee. Before each Board meeting, ExCo submits a consolidated Board Paper across all operating divisions, coordinated by the Group CEO's office. The Group CEO executes Board Paper resolutions and all underlying commercial and operational agreements:

Executive Committee (ExCo)

Composition & Voting: Investor Director (Chair, single ordinary vote), Investee Director (one vote), and Independent Director (one vote). The Executive Team participates to report with zero voting rights.

Decision Threshold: Supermajority (≥ 2/3 affirmative votes).

Cadence: Bi-weekly.

Scope: Operational oversight across all three operating divisions. Ratifies the agreed capital allocation post-setup.

Audit & Risk (AR-TP)

Composition: Investee Director (Chair), Investor Director, Group COO (Secretary), Group CFO, and external auditor.

Cadence: Quarterly.

Scope: Financial audit oversight, statutory tax compliance, verification of the transfer pricing cost-plus formula, and veterinary quarantine compliance.

Executive Management

A single Group Executive Team holds joint operational authority. The Group CEO reports to the Board and committees. The Group COO and Group CFO report directly to the Group CEO.

Decision Authority

Authority cascades from the Board down through operational committees to daily management:

  • Board Supermajority Consent: Fundamental strategic matters require four out of five board votes (e.g., approving the Annual Operating Plan, unbudgeted CAPEX over USD 100k, hiring C-suite, taking debt over USD 250k).
  • Audit & Risk Oversight: Reviews and approves annual transfer pricing margins under tax law, tax filings, credit terms beyond 30 days, and validates export quarantine protocols.
  • Executive Committee Sign-Off: Requires a supermajority of voting members. ExCo formalizes the capital allocation post-setup, approves unbudgeted operational spending ($25k–$75k), locks in cattle purchase prices, and approves logistics contracts up to 90 days.
  • Management Authority: The Group CEO, COO, and CFO run day-to-day operations. They approve budgeted expenses up to USD 25k, buy cattle using the agreed formula, and handle site-level hiring.
3.

Kenya OpCo

Proposed Execution

Operational deployment transitions the Investee's rangeland, yards, and herds into an active export pipeline across a defined Day T0 to Day T+90 execution schedule:

  • Operational Footprint Mobilized (T0) Feedlots (Kitengela/Narok), 500-acre fodder cultivation, 12,000-head breeding nucleus, and active domestic continuity (Farmers Choice).
  • Days T0 to T+30 (Setup & Allocation) ExCo ratifies the ~USD 10.0M allocation, locks the 10% reserve, transitions staff, and closes 25-year leases and statutory caveats.
  • Days T+30 to T+60 (Ingestion & Quarantine) Day-1 stock acquisition (USD 1.0M), feedlot-gate steer intake under Salam contracts, PEI quarantine testing, and groundbreaking for the modular Halal abattoir.
  • Days T+60 to T+90 (Export & Settlement) Cattle reach export weights (~450kg), process through the abattoir, clear JKIA air-cargo to UAE OpCo, and commercial accounts settle via the cash waterfall.

Asset Integration

To avoid valuation disputes and asset conveyancing taxes, Kenya OpCo secures operational control through three contracts:

  • 25-Year Commercial Leases: Registered under the Kenya Land Registration Act over operational areas.
  • Statutory Caveats: Lodged at the Ministry of Lands barring third-party charges or sales during the venture term.
  • Facilities Use Agreement: Access to existing yards, bunks, boreholes, and bunkers against maintenance costs.
  • Feedlot-Gate Ingestion: Kenya OpCo buys 280–320 kg feeder steers via forward purchase contracts (Salam) at the gate; core breeding herds remain on the Investee's balance sheet.

UAE OpCo

Downstream Logistics

UAE OpCo leases temperature-controlled warehousing within KEZAD or JAFZA to stage chilled carcasses and frozen cuts upon port arrival, and deploys dedicated refrigerated vehicles for last-mile delivery to GCC clients.

Commercial Execution

UAE OpCo signs binding GCC off-take sales contracts, clears air and sea customs, and invoices buyers in local currencies (AED and SAR). It transacts with Kenya OpCo under an arm's-length Master Supply & Off-Take Agreement and established Transfer Pricing Agreement using a cost-plus formula compliant with KRA and UAE Corporate Tax rules.
