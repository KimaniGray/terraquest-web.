1.

Executive Summary

Project Ndegwa translates the original commercial vision of PineCrest Holdings (the Investee) into an institutional, Shariah-compliant, asset-backed deployment architecture for Golden Nile Trading LLC (the Investor). Capitalizing on preferential zero-tariff (0%) customs benefits under the Kenya-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), the platform establishes a vertically integrated protein aggregation, feed manufacturing, genetic multiplication, and export enterprise targeting the GCC protein import market and East African wholesale sectors.

The transaction executes across a unified Two-Phase Investment Architecture establishing a USD 100,000,000 Total Baseline Program (comprising symmetrical USD 50.0M commitments per phase, with 30.0% Accordion Investment Tranches providing expansion headroom up to an aggregate authorized envelope of USD 130,000,000):

  • Phase 1: Foundational Production Engine & Integrated Feed (Months 1–24 | USD 50,000,000 Baseline): Front-loads feed industrialization to secure live finishing costs at USD 180–205/MT (versus USD 340/MT open market). Deploys an on-site 15 MT/hr automated feed mill, a 4–5 MT/hr lucerne dehydrating and pelleting line, a mechanical soybean extrusion and pressing line (yielding 44–46% bypass protein cake and crude oil), a 13,000 MT bulk corrugated steel silo terminal (4x 2,500 MT maize flat bins + 2x 1,500 MT soya hopper bins, strictly excluding concrete silos) with a 30 MT/hr column dryer, feedlot expansions to 18,000 standing head, an on-site Bovine IVF & Embryo Transfer Cleanroom Laboratory (USD 800k) with full 5-stage IVEP cascade, an export Halal abattoir for beef, goat, and lamb with camel design accommodation (excluding poultry), 100% origin deboning, seam-cutting, and retail barrier packaging, and a commercial smokehouse unit monetizing 100% of trim into cooked deli meats and high-velocity "smokies".
  • Phase 2: Scaled Industrial Value Chain Expansion (Month 25 Onward | USD 50,000,000 Baseline): Multiplies enterprise margins across four high-moat agribusiness additions: (1) Bulk Steel Grain Terminal expansion to 30,000 MT via four additional 4,250 MT bins; (2) an industrial Direct Wet-Blue Leather Tannery (USD 4.5M) processing 100% of hides and skins for export; (3) secondary carcass holding chillers and a second blast freezer cell (USD 3.0M) doubling plant throughput; (4) a 10,000–20,000 acre Coastal Pre-Export Quarantine (PEQ) Staging Ranch near Mombasa (USD 8.5M); and (5) a specialized asset-light Contracted Charter Logistics Business (USD 10.0M revolving freight float) securing time charters, COAs, and ACMI air cargo freighters with strictly zero maritime vessel or aircraft ownership on the joint venture balance sheet.

The joint venture is governed as a Shariah-compliant Musharaka partnership structured under English Common Law in the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM), capitalized with a nominal USD 50,000 paid-up ordinary share capital maintaining absolute, non-dilutive 50/50 ordinary voting parity between Investor and Investee across all drawdowns. Corporate authority sits with a lean 4-member Board of Directors (two Investee appointees and two Investor appointees) operating without third-party kingmaker bottlenecks, supported by a bilateral sponsor Executive Committee (ExCo).

Operational execution is divided between Kenya OpCo and UAE OpCo:

  • Kenya OpCo holds all operational assets, facilities, leases, and biological stock in Kenya. It operates under 25-year commercial registered leases over feedlot yards and fodder acreage, directly owns the abattoir, cold chain, 15 MT/hr feed mill, bulk steel silos, Bovine IVF laboratory, and tannery capitalized under IAS 16, manages the 18,000-head feedlot finishing yards, and procures livestock intake and grains under Shariah-compliant Salam and Murabaha contracts. 100% of seam-boning, portioning, retail skin-packaging, and cooked charcuterie is executed at origin in Kenya to maximize domestic labor cost advantages and eliminate air-freighting waste bones to the Gulf.
  • UAE OpCo operates as an asset-light commercial sales, trading, and distribution subsidiary in the UAE. It signs binding GCC off-take sales contracts, clears import customs under CEPA zero-tariff preferences, manages leased third-party (3PL) bonded cold-storage staging in KEZAD/JAFZA, coordinates last-mile refrigerated fulfillment, and invoices institutional supermarket and foodservice accounts in local currencies (AED and SAR).

The two operating companies transact under an arm's-length Master Supply & Off-Take Agreement and Transfer Pricing Agreement utilizing an economic cost-plus formula supported by independent benchmarking studies compliant with Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) and UAE Corporate Tax transfer pricing regulations.

Deployed capital is 100% non-recourse to the Investee's foundational breeding herds and ancestral rangeland titles. Under no circumstances does capital recovery, Accordion drawdown, or Shariah financing dilute the equal 50/50 ordinary voting parity established on Day 1.

  • Capital Deployment: Project capital across Phase 1 and Phase 2 is deployed outside nominal equity through dedicated Islamic facilities (Istisna'a, Salam, and Murabaha) and recovered through Tier 2 of the Re-Payment Waterfall.

Board Governance

The Board of Directors of UAE InvestCo is constituted of four (4) members: two (2) appointed by the Investee and two (2) appointed by the Investor. The Chairmanship rotates annually between the Principals with no casting vote. Quorum strictly mandates the presence of at least one director appointed by each Principal, eliminating outside kingmaker influence and protecting bilateral 50/50 control under all scenarios.

Operating Divisions

Execution across UAE InvestCo, Kenya OpCo, and UAE OpCo is organized into three focused corporate divisions:

  • Commercial & Trading: Seated under the Group CEO. Drives GCC off-take agreements, institutional retail and supermarket accounts, HORECA programs, export pricing matrices, CEPA optimization, customer credit, and regional marketing.
  • Agribusiness & Operations: Directed by the Group COO. End-to-end management of feedlot finishing yards (18k head), the 15 MT/hr feed mill and steel grain terminal, the Bovine IVF cleanroom laboratory, abattoir dressing floors, 100% origin deboning, and cold-chain integrity.
  • Finance & Legal: Directed by the Group CFO. Financial controls, rolling 13-week cash flow modeling, transfer pricing compliance, AAOIFI Shariah auditing, and waterfall sweeps.

Committee Structure

Governance is supported by two standing committees. Operational decisions are expedited through bilateral sponsor consensus:

Executive Committee (ExCo)

Composition: One (1) Investor Representative + One (1) Investee Representative. The Group CEO, Group COO, and Group CFO participate without voting rights.

Decision Rule: Bilateral sponsor consensus.

Scope: Routine operational oversight across all facilities, cattle intake formulas, and line-item variances within Account 3 up to ±15%.

Audit, Risk & Transfer Pricing (AR-TP)

Composition: Investee Director (Chair), Investor Director, Group COO, Group CFO, and statutory auditor.

Scope: Financial audit oversight, statutory KRA and UAE Corporate Tax filings, transfer pricing benchmark verification, and DVS/MoCCaE export biosecurity compliance.

Decision Authority

Authority cascades cleanly from the Board down through ExCo to the executive management team:

  • Board Supermajority Consent: Fundamental strategic Reserved Matters require the affirmative vote of at least three (3) directors, including at least one director appointed by the Investee and one appointed by the Investor (e.g., Annual Operating Budget, unbudgeted CAPEX > USD 100k, external debt > USD 250k, commercial poultry carve-out approval, and reducing the 10% Cash Liquidity Reserve).
  • Audit & Risk Oversight: Reviews and approves annual transfer pricing economic studies, statutory filings, customer credit terms exceeding 30 days, and export health accreditations.
  • Executive Committee Sign-Off: Bilateral sponsor consensus ratifies Drawdown Notices, line-item adjustments within Account 3 up to ±15%, unbudgeted operational expenses ($25k–$75k), and cattle buying formulas.
  • Management Authority: The Group CEO, COO, and CFO run daily commercial operations, approving budgeted operational expenses up to USD 25,000 and managing pastoralist supply lines.

Kenya OpCo

Proposed Execution

Operational deployment activates the Investee's foundational assets, rangelands, and pastoralist corridors into a high-throughput protein engine across a disciplined Day T0 to Day T+90 execution roadmap:

  • Operational Footprint Mobilized (T0) PineCrest feedlots (Kitengela/Narok), 500-acre irrigated Narok lucerne hub, foundation Boran breeding herds, and active domestic sales continuity (Farmers Choice) mobilized under Kenya OpCo operational umbrella.
  • Days T0 to T+30 (Corporate Setup & Professional Close) Settlement of Stage 1 Structuring Fee, execution of definitive SHA and Shariah Facility Contracts, registration of 25-year commercial leases and statutory caveats, and joint appointment of independent engineering design firm and Quantity Surveyor (QS).
  • Days T+30 to T+60 (Detailed Engineering, Feeder Intake & Groundbreaking) Board ratification of Stage 6 Final QS Costing, Day-1 commercial cattle buyout (USD 3.0M), feedlot expansion to 18,000 standing head under forward Salam contracts, and simultaneous groundbreaking for the 15 MT/hr feed mill, 13,000 MT steel silo terminal, Bovine IVF biotechnology laboratory, and modular Halal abattoir.
  • Days T+60 to T+90 (Feed Milling, Genetic Ingestion & Export Go-Live) Commissioning of 15 MT/hr feed mill and lucerne pelleting plant, initial embryo harvesting and transfers in IVF laboratory, abattoir commissioning with 100% origin deboning, and launch of boxed vacuum-packed beef and smoked deli cuts to the UAE.

Asset Integration & Land Ring-Fencing

To eliminate valuation friction and property conveyance taxes, Kenya OpCo secures operational control through legal and operational covenants that strictly protect the Investee's foundational balance sheet:

  • 25-Year Commercial Leases: Registered under the Kenya Land Registration Act over feedlot yards, fodder acreage, abattoir, feed mill, and silo sites.
  • Statutory Caveats: Lodged at the Ministry of Lands barring unauthorized third-party charges, mortgages, or alienation during the venture term.
  • Facilities Use Agreement: Direct access to existing yards, bunks, boreholes, and fodder acreage against routine maintenance and operating costs.
  • Feedlot-Gate Ingestion: Kenya OpCo buys feeder cattle via forward purchase contracts (Salam) at the yard gate; foundational breeding herds and land titles remain 100% on the Investee's balance sheet under absolute non-recourse covenants.

UAE OpCo

Downstream Logistics & Asset-Light Distribution

UAE OpCo executes an asset-light destination model. It manages leased, bonded temperature-controlled cold storage within KEZAD (Abu Dhabi) or Dubai Wholesale City (JAFZA) to cross-dock boxed retail cuts and frozen inventory upon arrival at DXB/DWC or Jebel Ali. It contracts dedicated refrigerated fleet operators (3PL) for last-mile delivery to GCC supermarket chains, foodservice distributors, and institutional catering accounts, avoiding brick-and-mortar overseas CAPEX.

Commercial Execution

UAE OpCo signs binding off-take sales contracts across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and wider GCC, clears customs under CEPA zero-tariff import preferences, and invoices customers in local currencies (AED and SAR). It transacts with Kenya OpCo under an arm's-length Master Supply & Off-Take Agreement and Transfer Pricing Agreement using an economic cost-plus formula compliant with KRA and UAE Corporate Tax rules.
