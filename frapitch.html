Market Context

The Food Reserve Agency (FRA) is statutorily mandated to administer the national food reserve. Under this mandate, the primary purposes of the reserve are strictly defined as:

  • Ensure a reliable supply of designated commodities for the country.
  • Meet local shortfalls in the supply of a designated commodity.
  • Meet food emergencies caused by drought, flood, or natural disaster.
  • Correct supply problems resulting from price manipulation or monopolistic trading.

To execute this heavy statutory mandate, the FRA has developed a National Maize Program targeting a strategic buffer of 500,000 to 1,000,000 Metric Tons, with the capacity to procure an additional 1,000,000 Metric Tons for market stabilization. Executing this physical accumulation requires a predictable, baseline liquidity facility of USD 500 Million annually.

Currently, the Agency executes this capital-intensive mandate without a structured, independent funding mechanism. This gap creates severe fiscal constraints for the Exchequer and the FRA, directly risking the viability of the program, national food security, and economic support for over 1,000,000 small-scale farmers.

Unplanned Exchequer Dependency

In 2024, the Treasury was forced to allocate ZMW 2.85 Billion in direct government grants to support the Agency's operations. This structural dependency drains liquidity that the Ministry of Finance could otherwise deploy toward priority national development sectors.

Intra-Government Liquidity Constraints

To fulfill its mandate to meet national food emergencies, the FRA routinely releases grain to state institutions (such as DMMU, ZNS, and ZCS). Without a pre-funded settlement mechanism, this generates massive, unpaid receivables that paralyze the Agency's working capital for subsequent purchasing seasons.

Inventory & Audit Vulnerabilities

The reliance on traditional storage solutions prevents the State from maintaining fully bankable, internationally certified visibility over the national food reserve, misaligning the Agency with global sovereign governance and audit standards.

5-Year Vision

To permanently resolve these constraints, TerraQuest Investments will structure and syndicate a USD 400 Million institutional facility (Tsavo Resilience Food Fund 1). Operating alongside an optimized USD 100 Million Government contribution, this establishes a fully funded, 5-year capital structure built entirely on predictability.

Predictable Hard Currency

We deliver both the initial USD 400 Million capital injection and a structured regional export market. This guarantees USD-denominated off-take contracts for Zambia's surplus maize, creating a powerful, predictable foreign exchange earner for the national economy.

Predictable Treasury Relief

Anchoring long-term institutional capital permanently removes the FRA from the government’s operational balance sheet. This predictable structure halts the cycle of erratic bailouts, freeing billions of Kwacha for strategic fiscal reallocation.

Predictable Rural Incomes

Guaranteeing absolute funding year-on-year ensures over 1,000,000 small-scale farmers receive prompt, guaranteed payments upon delivery. This predictability stabilizes rural economies and fundamentally incentivizes scaled agricultural production.

Foreign Direct Investment

A fully capitalized procurement program acts as a sovereign magnet for an additional USD 500 Million in foreign direct investment. This follow-on capital is earmarked to modernize logistics, construct climate-controlled bulk silos, and deploy critical agricultural power infrastructure.
