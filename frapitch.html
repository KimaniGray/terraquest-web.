MARKET CONTEXT

Statutory Mandate

Under the Food Reserve Act, the Food Reserve Agency (FRA) administers the National Strategic Food Reserve. The statutory purpose is to:

  • Ensure a reliable supply of designated commodities for the country.
  • Meet local shortfalls in supply.
  • Handle food emergencies caused by drought, floods, or natural disasters.
  • Correct problems resulting from price manipulation or monopolistic practices.

To fulfill these objectives, the FRA developed a National Maize Program to create a strategic reserve of 500,000 to 1,000,000 Metric Tons, and purchase an additional 1,000,000 Metric Tons for market stabilization. Executing this requires predictable capital of USD 500 Million year-on-year.

Structural Gaps

Currently, the Agency executes this highly capital-intensive mandate without an independent, structured funding mechanism. This gap creates severe fiscal constraints for the government, risking the viability of the program, national food security, and vital support for over 1,000,000 small-scale farmers.

Exchequer Dependency

In 2024, the Treasury issued ZMW 2.85 Billion in direct grants, severely draining funds from other national development priorities.

Liquidity Constraints

The FRA routinely releases grain to state bodies during emergencies. Without pre-funding, these receivables wipe out seasonal working capital.

Audit Vulnerabilities

Traditional state storage lacks certified inventory management, preventing the State from maintaining a bankable, auditable national reserve.
