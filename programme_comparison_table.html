3. Summary of the Programme
Segregation & Ring-Fencing Mechanics
Segregation Terminology "Series Transaction"
Uses the prefix "Series" for all related assets/liabilities.		 "Transaction"
Drops the "Series" prefix.
Legal Definition of Segregation A distinct series of contracts where "Participating Assets and Notes constitute a segregated subset of assets and liabilities of the Issuer".
Naming Conventions (The Prefix Rule) To prevent cross-contamination in a highly generic programme, everything is prefixed: Series Assets, Series Liabilities, Series Guarantee, Series Priority of Payments. Because the platform is specific to agricultural assets, the prefix is dropped: Assets, Liabilities, Guarantee, Priority of Payments.
4. Assets, Liabilities & Pool Structure
Asset Eligibility & Pool Structure Broad, Open-Ended & Dynamic Pool: Consists of general corporate bonds, commercial paper, syndicated loans, derivatives, and cash. Because an Asset Manager may be appointed to actively trade the portfolio, the pool composition can change dynamically. Exact criteria are defined per-Series in the Applicable Pricing Supplement. Highly Specific, Physical & Static Pool: Consists of agricultural asset-backed loans, real property (farmland leases), biological assets (crops/livestock), and farming equipment. Typically structured as a static pool of physical assets or specialized leases. Criteria are defined per-Transaction in the Applicable Transaction Supplement.
Legal Title & Transfer Mechanism Financial Transfer: Achieved via standard assignment of loan rights, novation, or electronic transfer of uncertificated bonds into the SPV's Central Securities Depository (CSD — Strate) account. Physical & Property Transfer: Often requires physical delivery (equipment), registration of leasehold/title rights at the Deeds Office, or specialized agricultural pledge agreements.
Specific Liabilities Defined (The Obligations) Series Liabilities: The Notes issued under that specific Series, plus standard financial obligations like International Swaps and Derivatives Association (ISDA) swap payments, hedging costs, and corporate administrator fees for that Series only. Transaction Liabilities: The Notes issued under that specific Transaction, plus unique agricultural operational liabilities (e.g., crop insurance premiums, farm management Service Level Agreement (SLA) fees, and community trust payouts).
Limited Recourse & Ring-Fencing Scope Zero cross-pollination. The holders of Notes issued in respect of one [Series] Transaction have recourse only to the Participating Assets of that specific [Series] Transaction. They have no legal claim to any other assets held by the Issuer SPV for other transactions.

Interest & Cash-Flow Profiles Supports Fixed Rate, Floating Rate (Johannesburg Interbank Average Rate — JIBAR-linked), Zero-Coupon, Mixed-Rate, and Index-Linked Notes tailored to corporate debt or capital-markets yields. How Note Classes & Tranches Differ Alphabetical Class Nomenclature (e.g., Class A / B / C):
• Master Memorandum: Establishes that Notes in a Series may be divided into multiple Classes (typically designated alphabetically as Class A, Class B, Class C) ranking pari passu within each Class, without hardcoding fixed spreads.
• Pricing Supplement (Deal Level): Defines the exact class distinctions for that Series: (1) Waterfall Priority (Class A paid before B and C), (2) Coupon Margin (step-up JIBAR spreads for junior classes), (3) Security Split (e.g., Class A secured vs. Class C unsecured), and (4) Controlling Class Rights (senior Noteholders direct enforcement).		 Functional Seniority Nomenclature (Senior / Mezzanine / Junior):
• Master Memorandum: Does not use "Class A / B / C" labels; instead, it structures tranches by functional rank as Senior Notes, Mezzanine Notes, and Junior / Subordinated Notes.
• Transaction & Pricing Supplements (Deal Level): Define exact tranche mechanics: (1) First-Loss Absorption (Junior/Subordinated Notes absorb crop/equipment write-downs before Mezzanine or Senior Notes), (2) Sequential Payout (strict top-down payout from harvest collections), (3) Yield Differential (higher margins for agricultural risk), and (4) Controlling Senior Rights (Senior Noteholders direct physical asset repossession).

Step 4 — Settlement & Funding: Uncertificated Notes are issued and settled electronically via Strate against investor cash, which repays the Warehouse Provider and acquires the Series Assets. 7. Credit Enhancement Agreement & Mechanics
Counterparty / Provider Credit Enhancement Provider (RMB, the Asset Originator, subordinated Note investors, or third-party insurers/off-takers).
1. Tranche Subordination (The Waterfall) Notes within a Series Transaction are tranched into alphabetical classes (e.g., Class A, Class B, Class C). Losses are absorbed strictly bottom-up: junior classes take 100% of principal write-downs before Class A Noteholders lose a single cent. Notes within a Transaction are tranched by functional seniority into Senior Notes, Mezzanine Notes, and Junior / Subordinated Notes. Losses are absorbed strictly bottom-up: Junior/Subordinated tranches take 100% of write-downs before Mezzanine or Senior Noteholders absorb any loss.
2. Over-Collateralisation (OC) The SPV acquires corporate debt or bonds with a nominal face value exceeding the principal value of the issued Notes, creating an immediate equity buffer against borrower defaults. Higher Haircuts Required: Because physical farm equipment and crops depreciate or face weather volatility, Agri Harvest typically requires significantly deeper over-collateralisation (OC) ratios (e.g., R130m of farm assets backing R100m of Notes).
3. Excess Spread The positive margin between the interest/lease yield generated by the underlying Asset Pool and the coupon paid out on the Notes (plus SPV fees). This surplus cash acts as the first line of defense to absorb monthly defaults before touching principal reserves.
4. Reserve Funds & Subordinated Loans
Credit Enhancement Agreement		 Funded via a dedicated cash reserve account or a subordinated loan from RMB/the originator, sized to cover expected corporate default rates and rating agency stress tests. Often includes a dedicated Maintenance / Crop Loss Reserve Fund funded upfront to buffer seasonal cash flow dips, equipment repairs, or insurance deductibles between harvests.
5. Third-Party Guarantees & Offtake Security May utilize monoline guarantees, bank letters of credit, or Credit Default Swaps (CDS) to wrap specific corporate exposures. Relies heavily on ring-fenced off-take agreements (binding contracts with major food processors/retailers to buy the harvest) and comprehensive multi-peril crop/asset insurance ceded directly to the Security SPV.

18. Risk Factors
Shared Structural & Securitisation Risks
Limited Recourse & Ring-Fencing Risk Noteholders have recourse only to the segregated assets of their specific [Series] Transaction. If those assets underperform or default, investors cannot claim against other silos or FirstRand/RMB.
Commingling & Insolvency Risk If the Servicer or Account Bank becomes insolvent while holding cash collections prior to sweeping them into the SPV's ring-fenced account, those funds may be temporarily frozen or commingled with the insolvent estate.
Secondary Market Illiquidity Although Notes may be listed on the JSE Interest Rate Market, there is no guarantee of an active secondary market. Investors may have to hold Notes until final legal maturity.
Regulatory & Tax Changes Changes in South African banking regulations (Banks Act Securitisation Exemption Notice), exchange control rules, or withholding tax laws could trigger early redemption or reduce net investor yields.
Programme-Specific Asset & Operational Risks
Underlying Asset Performance Risk Corporate Credit & Market Risk: Exposure to corporate borrower defaults, credit rating downgrades, interest rate/basis mismatches, and mark-to-market volatility on financial instruments. Biological, Climate & Commodity Risk: Direct exposure to droughts, floods, crop disease, livestock mortality, and fluctuations in agricultural commodity prices impacting farm cash flows.
Servicer / Asset Manager Replacement Risk Active Trading & Reinvestment Risk: Where an Asset Manager actively trades the pool, poor reinvestment decisions or difficulty replacing a defaulting manager can erode portfolio yield. Physical Recovery & Specialist Transition Risk: If a farm operator or agri-servicer defaults, finding a qualified replacement with specialized agricultural expertise to maintain biological assets without value destruction is difficult.
Collateral Realisation & Enforcement Financial Liquidation: Security enforcement primarily involves liquidating tradable bonds, enforcing financial cessions, or closing out ISDA swap positions. Physical Repossession & Land Tenure: Enforcing security requires physical repossession of farming equipment, navigating Deeds Office transfers, and managing land reform or community leasehold disputes.

20. Terms, Covenants & Regulatory Compliance
Negative Covenants & Bankruptcy Remoteness Strict ring-fenced ("RF") restrictions in the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) and Terms & Conditions: the Issuer SPV cannot incur unauthorised debt, grant unpermitted security, merge/amalgamate, declare dividends out of ring-fenced assets, or have employees.
Non-Petition & Limited Recourse Clauses All Transaction Parties and Noteholders contractually agree not to institute winding-up, liquidation, or business rescue proceedings against the Issuer SPV or Security SPV for at least 1–2 years after the final Note across all silos has been redeemed.
Events of Default (Silo-Isolated) Series Event of Default: Non-payment of Series interest/principal (after grace period), breach of Series covenants, or insolvency of the Issuer. Triggering default on Series 1 does not cross-default Series 2. Transaction Event of Default: Non-payment of Transaction interest/principal, breach of asset maintenance/insurance covenants, or unwaived off-take termination. Strictly isolated to that specific Transaction silo.
Regulatory & Tax Framework South African Securitisation & Tax Rules: Structured in compliance with the South African Banks Act (Securitisation Exemption Notice), JSE Debt Listings Requirements (for listed tranches), South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Exchange Control regulations, and South African Income Tax Act withholding tax provisions on interest.
Subscription & Selling Restrictions Institutional & Cross-Border Restrictions: Notes may not be offered to the general public in South Africa in contravention of the Companies Act (minimum denomination ZAR 1,000,000 for unlisted/private placements), and are subject to United States (U.S.) Regulation S, United Kingdom (U.K.), and European Economic Area (EEA) selling restrictions.
